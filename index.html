Step 4 · Carrier registration

Every US carrier requires this before your number can text.

Sixty seconds, and we submit it today. This is the one screen that asks for a tax ID. It's what the carriers check your business against.

Exactly as it reads on your EIN letter. Often that's your trading name plus LLC or Inc., and for a sole proprietor it's frequently your own name. Carrier registration is rejected on a single mismatched character, so it's worth reading off the paperwork rather than memory.

Business type
EIN, your federal tax ID

Nine digits. Never your Social Security number. We don't accept one, and the carriers don't want it either.

Don't have an EIN?

Most sole proprietors don't. There are two ways forward and the difference is worth a minute.

Getting one takes about 10 minutes. The IRS issues an EIN online at irs.gov and shows you the number on screen straight away. It's free, and it's what most banks ask for when you open a business account.

Without one, you still get all of it. Texting from your business number, missed-call text-back, review requests, and your reactivation and referral campaigns. Promotional messages are allowed either way.

What's different: the carriers cap a business with no EIN at one campaign, one phone number, and about 1,000 messages a day to T-Mobile customers, roughly a third of US phones. Everything shares that pool: missed-call replies, review requests, and campaigns. A first campaign to a few hundred past customers fits comfortably. A bigger list gets paced over several days instead of going out at once.

The part that matters most: those limits are fixed. Businesses registered with an EIN earn higher limits as they build a track record. Businesses without one never do. You can switch later, but it means registering again and waiting on another carrier review.

Either way, we text a verification code to your personal mobile. It can't be a VoIP or app-based number, so not your office line.

Keep going, this is saved. We'll text the link to your mobile the moment you finish, so you can do it when you're off the road. If you'd rather start now, it's irs.gov, and this page will still be here when you come back.
